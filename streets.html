Zone by zone

The chips are literal street names. Put them in the search box, or check a listing's address against them before you book.

11eOberkampf / Saint-MaurBest value

Search here first. Bars and bistros run the length of Oberkampf and Jean-Pierre Timbaud; Saint-Maur and Parmentier are the quieter residential streets one block off, which is where the value is.

  • Avenue Parmentier
  • Rue Jean-Pierre Timbaud
  • Rue Oberkampf
  • Rue Popincourt
  • Rue Saint-Maur
  • Rue de la Folie-Méricourt
11eRoquette / BastilleBest value

The Bastille side. Rue de la Roquette and Rue de Charonne are the spines; Keller, Basfroi and Trousseau are quiet side streets a two-minute walk from both. Rue de Lappe is loud until 2am — avoid a window on it.

  • Rue Basfroi
  • Rue Faidherbe
  • Rue Keller
  • Rue Paul Bert
  • Rue Sedaine
  • Rue Trousseau
  • Rue de Charonne
  • Rue de la Roquette
12eAligre / FaubourgBest value

Cheapest of the three and the most residential. Rue d'Aligre has the covered market and the open-air one six mornings a week. Rue de Cotte and Rue de Cîteaux are quiet; Rue de Charenton carries traffic.

  • Rue Crozatier
  • Rue Traversière
  • Rue d'Aligre
  • Rue de Charenton
  • Rue de Cotte
  • Rue de Cîteaux
3eHaut MaraisWorth stretching for

Real Marais, upper half. Rue de Bretagne runs past the Marché des Enfants Rouges, the oldest covered market in Paris. Charlot and Poitou are the pretty quiet ones. Everything south of Rue Rambuteau jumps in price.

  • Rue Charlot
  • Rue Vieille du Temple
  • Rue de Bretagne
  • Rue de Poitou
  • Rue de Turenne
10eCanal, south & east bankWorth stretching for

Only the water's south and east bank. Quai de Valmy and Quai de Jemmapes face the canal; Beaurepaire and Lancry run back toward République. Stop at Rue de la Grange aux Belles — north of that you are drifting into the next block.

  • Quai de Jemmapes
  • Quai de Valmy
  • Rue Beaurepaire
  • Rue de Lancry
  • Rue de la Grange aux Belles
10eStations wedge — avoidAvoid

The cheap listings you will be tempted by. Boulevard de Strasbourg, the north end of Faubourg Saint-Denis and Château d'Eau sit between two mainline stations. Fine in daylight, not what you want walking back at midnight.

  • Boulevard de Magenta
  • Boulevard de Strasbourg
  • Rue du Château d'Eau
  • Rue du Faubourg Saint-Denis

Two things to hold onto. A zone is a walking radius, not a fence — the halo is roughly a hundred metres either side of the named streets, so a listing one block outside is usually still fine. And the dashed red wedge is the one hard rule here: it sits between the Gare du Nord and Gare de l'Est, it is where the cheapest listings in central Paris cluster, and the saving is not worth it.
